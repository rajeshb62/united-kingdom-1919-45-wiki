Democratic Ideals and Reality

Democratic Ideals and Reality

Jul 11, 20266 min read

Summary

Halford Mackinder’s Democratic Ideals and Reality is the founding text of modern geopolitics, introducing his famous Heartland thesis. Written in the final months of World War I and published in 1919, the book argues that the age of Columbian exploration is over and that the world has become a closed geopolitical system dominated by the vast Eurasian “World-Island.” Mackinder warns the democratic powers that the post-war settlement must prevent any single power — particularly Germany or Russia — from dominating the Heartland (roughly Eastern Europe and Central Asia), lest it build a world empire. His core dictum — “Who rules East Europe commands the Heartland; who rules the Heartland commands the World-Island; who rules the World-Island commands the World” — became one of the most influential geopolitical axioms of the twentieth century.

Source

Mackinder, Halford John. Democratic Ideals and Reality: A Study in the Politics of Reconstruction. New York: Henry Holt and Company, 1919.

Chapters

Preface

Dated 1st February 1919. Mackinder explains that the book’s ideas were first developed in his 1904 paper “The Geographical Pivot of History” and in a 1905 article on “Man-power as a Measure of National and Imperial Strength.” The war has confirmed, not shaken, his earlier views.

Chapter I — Perspective (p. 3)

The future and peace; causes of past wars; growth of opposing interests; danger of merely juridical conceptions of the League of Nations; the possibility of a World-Tyranny; the problem stated.

Chapter II — Social Momentum (p. 8)

Democratic Idealism, its successive tragedies, and its relation to Reality; the Economic Reality of the “Going Concern”; the organizers of Going Concerns; the emergence of organizers from Revolution; the organizer and social discipline; the great Organizer is the great Realist; democratic prejudice against Experts; the organizer thinks strategically; Napoleon; Bismarck; the strategical mentality of Prussia; “Kultur” and strategy; the German war map; strategical thought in Economics; Democracy thinks ethically; “No annexations, no indemnities”; the necessity of reckoning with both geographical and economic Reality.

Chapter III — The Seaman’s Point of View (p. 38)

The Unity of the Ocean, the first geographical Reality; consequences not yet fully accepted; a historical view; contending river-powers in Egypt; the Nile “closed” by land-power; contending sea-powers in the Mediterranean; the Mediterranean “closed” by land-power; the Latin Peninsula as a sea-base; the encompassing of the World-Promontory by sea-power from the Latin peninsular base; division within the Latin European Peninsula; the opportunity for sea-power from the lesser but insular sea-base of Britain; of sea-bases in general; of sea-power in the Great War; the World-Island; the ultimate base of sea-power.

Chapter IV — The Landsman’s Point of View (p. 90)

The World-Island seen from within; the Heartland physically defined; the other Natural Regions; the Arabian Centerland; the mobile riders and the plowmen; the Arab bid for World Empire; the Steppes-belt; the Tartar invasions and their consequences; the Tibetan Heights and the N.W. entries to China and India; the open access from the Heartland to Arabia and to Europe; the Heartland strategically defined; the Black Sea basin included; the Baltic basin included; the Heartland as real a physical fact as the World-Island; the ultimate citadel of land-power.

Chapter V — The Rivalry of Empires (p. 141)

The Cossack advance over the Heartland; the Russian Homeland sharply delimited; the real Europe divided into East and West Europe; history of the relations of East and West Europe; their fundamental opposition and essential difference; German and Slav in East Europe; Trafalgar and the two streams of history; Britain and the Not-Europe; East Europe really within the Heartland; British and French policy in the Nineteenth Century; the Great War caused by the German attempt to control East Europe and the Heartland; the Economic Reality of organized man-power — the Going Concern; Political Economy and National Economy; the great Economic change of 1878; the German policy of stimulating growth of man-power to occupy the Heartland; Laissez-faire also a policy of Empire; the clash of the two policies as the inevitable consequence of two Going Concerns.

Chapter VI — The Freedom of Nations (p. 183)

We won the War but were nearly defeated; the hypothetical German Heartland Empire; the Heartland as the persistent geographical threat to world liberty; Germany’s mistake of the Western offensive; Hamburg and the man-power policy; the necessity of dividing East Europe and the Heartland into three, not two, State-systems; the peoples of the Middle Tier; feasibility of the League of Nations if this is done; no predominant partner; reckoning with Going Concerns; reasonable equality of power among members of the League; certain strategical positions of world importance; the Going Concern in the future and the unequal growth of Nations; the ideal of the Independent Nation of balanced economic development; tragedies of the Going Concern; the policy of truly free nations which makes for Peace.

Chapter VII — The Freedom of Men (p. 226)

Whether men and women will be more free in such free Nations; the need of basing organization within the Nation on localities; the alternative organization based on nation-wide classes and interests leads inevitably to international war of classes; the ideal of balanced provinces within balanced nations; such organization gives greatest opportunity to greatest number of men; the cause of the Nationality movement; opposed to undue centralization; fraternal nations must be balanced economically and formed of fraternal provinces; Fraternity depends on controlling the development of Going Concerns.

Postscript (p. 252)

The recent General Election, its meaning in a world setting; of the saving virtue of Neighborliness.

Appendix (p. 257)

Note on an Incident at the Quai d’Orsay, 25th January, 1919.
