Annual Register, 1935

Jul 14, 20268 min read

The Annual Register for 1935 (Vol. 177)

Part I — English History

Chapter I — The Means Test Muddle and Increased Arms Expenditure

The year opened with the National Government still firmly in power but facing growing discontent over the Means Test and unemployment policy. Mr. Lloyd George launched his “New Deal” proposals at Bangor on January 17, calling for a Statutory Council to prepare reconstruction schemes, but the Government remained cautious. The White Paper on Defence (March) provoked German anger, while conscription was announced in Germany and Britain protested. The civil and military estimates reflected growing rearmament, and a Labour vote of censure over the Means Test was defeated. A new Housing Bill and the Government of India Bill advanced through Parliament.

Chapter II — The Silver Jubilee

The Stresa Conference (April 12) brought Britain, France and Italy together to consider relations with Germany following Hitler’s announcement of conscription. Sir John Simon and Mr. Eden reported on their European tours. The Budget was presented by Neville Chamberlain. The Silver Jubilee of King George V was celebrated with great enthusiasm across the country in May, with loyal addresses and national festivities. The Anglo-German Naval Agreement (June) permitted Germany to build up to 35% of British tonnage, causing resentment in France and Italy. Mr. Baldwin succeeded Ramsay MacDonald as Prime Minister on June 7. The Government of India Bill passed the Lords. The Peace Ballot results showed strong public support for the League of Nations.

Chapter III — Britain’s Lead to the League of Nations

Italy’s breach with the League over Abyssinia dominated the second half of the year. Britain took the lead at Geneva, with Sir Samuel Hoare and Mr. Eden advocating collective security and sanctions against Italy. The Cabinet recalled Parliament and the British fleet was strengthened in the Mediterranean. The Labour Party Conference and the Trades Union Congress supported the League. A General Election was held in November, resulting in a decisive victory for the National Government (Baldwin’s majority of 247). Sanctions against Italy commenced on November 18.

Chapter IV — The Government’s Volte-Face

Following the election, Mr. Baldwin reshuffled his Cabinet but retained its balance. Lord Londonderry was replaced by Lord Halifax. The Hoare-Laval proposals (December) — which offered Italy substantial territorial concessions in Abyssinia in exchange for peace — provoked a storm of public indignation when leaked to the press. Sir Samuel Hoare resigned as Foreign Secretary and was replaced by Mr. Eden. The Government reasserted its commitment to the League. The coal crisis loomed with a miners’ strike ballot. The Naval Conference in London opened with Japanese proposals.

Part I — Imperial History

Chapter I — Ireland: Northern Ireland

Northern Ireland’s finances improved through a revised Re-insurance Agreement under the Unemployment Acts. The Budget showed revenue of £12,552,000 against expenditure of £12,534,000. Sectarian rioting broke out in Belfast in May and July, the worst in many years, with nine persons shot dead and hundreds driven from their homes. The Transport Bill established the most complete road-rail co-ordination scheme yet devised. The Summary Jurisdiction Bill transferred judicial administration from JPs to Resident Magistrates.

Chapter II — Canada

Canada faced the deepening Depression. The new Prime Minister, Mr. R. B. Bennett, pursued protectionist policies and trade agreements with the United States. The Bank of Canada was established. Unemployment remained severe, and the Co-operative Commonwealth Federation (CCF) gained ground as a political force.

Chapter III — The Union of South Africa

General Hertzog’s government continued its policy of domestic consolidation. The gold standard was maintained. South Africa celebrated the Silver Jubilee. Tensions between the British and Afrikaner communities persisted over the country’s constitutional status and the question of participation in imperial defence.

Chapter IV — Australasia: Commonwealth of Australia and New Zealand

Australia’s economy showed signs of recovery under the Lyons government, with improved wool and wheat prices. New Zealand continued under the Coalition government of Mr. Forbes, with economic conditions gradually improving. Both dominions participated in the Silver Jubilee celebrations and maintained close ties with the imperial government.

Chapter V — India

The Government of India Bill, providing for provincial autonomy and a federal structure, completed its passage through Parliament. Debates were intense, with Mr. Churchill leading the opposition. The princes’ objections were addressed through amendments. The bill established the framework for elected provincial governments with responsible ministers, while reserving defence and foreign affairs to the Viceroy.

Part II — Foreign History

Chapter I — The League of Nations

The League faced its gravest crisis yet as Italy prepared to invade Abyssinia. Britain took the lead in pushing for collective action under the Covenant. The Council met in September and declared Italy the aggressor. Sanctions were imposed in November, though oil was excluded. The League’s authority and credibility hung in the balance.

Chapter II — France and Italy

France experienced four changes of government (Flandin, Bouisson, Laval), with a 12-billion-franc deficit and political instability. Pierre Laval pursued conciliation with Italy and sought to mediate the Abyssinian dispute. Italy under Mussolini rejected League arbitration, mobilised for the invasion of Abyssinia, and faced sanctions with defiance.

Chapter III — Germany and Austria

Germany under Hitler announced universal military conscription (March 16), repudiating the military clauses of Versailles. The Anglo-German Naval Agreement (June 18) was a major diplomatic coup for Berlin. The Nuremberg Laws institutionalised anti-Semitism. In Austria, Chancellor Schuschnigg struggled to maintain independence against Nazi pressure, with Italy distracted by Abyssinia.

Chapter IV — Soviet Russia and Eastern Europe

The Soviet Union signed a mutual assistance pact with France (May 2) and pursued collective security through the League, which it had joined in 1934. Stalin’s purges began to take shape. Poland maintained a delicate balancing act between Germany and the Soviet Union. The Little Entente (Czechoslovakia, Rumania, Yugoslavia) faced internal strains.

Chapter V — Lesser States of Western and Northern Europe

Belgium restored conscription in response to German rearmament. The Netherlands and Switzerland maintained neutrality. Spain saw the Lerroux government face increasing instability, with the Left gaining ground. The Nordic states co-operated closely on economic and foreign policy.

Chapter VI — The Middle East

Iran changed its name from Persia (formally requested in March). Iraq achieved a new Anglo-Iraqi treaty strengthening its sovereignty. Palestine experienced renewed Arab-Jewish tensions over land purchases and immigration. Syria remained under French mandate with nationalist agitation. Saudi Arabia consolidated under Ibn Saud.

Chapter VII — The Far East: China and Japan

Japan continued its domination of Manchuria and pressed for further concessions in North China. China under the Kuomintang struggled to present a united front against Japanese encroachment. The Soviet-Japanese tension over border issues persisted.

Chapter VIII — Morocco and Egypt

Morocco remained under French and Spanish administration with nationalist stirrings. Egypt, nominally independent under the 1930 constitution, faced Anglo-Egyptian treaty negotiations over the status of the Sudan and British military presence. The Wafd party pressed for full sovereignty.

Chapter IX — America: The United States and Latin America

The United States under President Roosevelt continued the New Deal. The Works Progress Administration (WPA) was created, and the Social Security Act and Wagner Act were passed. The US remained isolationist but co-operated on the Stresa front and naval limitation. In Latin America, the Chaco War between Bolivia and Paraguay concluded. Brazil experienced political unrest.

Part II — Chronicle of Events in 1935

Day-by-day record of notable events throughout the year, including the Silver Jubilee celebrations, the Stresa Conference, the Anglo-German Naval Agreement, the General Election, the Italo-Abyssinian crisis, the Hoare-Laval proposals, and key parliamentary debates.

Retrospect of Literature, Art, and Science in 1935

Literature (by J. Isaacs): Notable works included T.S. Eliot’s Murder in the Cathedral, Vera Brittain’s Testament of Youth, and James Hilton’s Lost Horizon. Art (by W.T. Whitley): The Royal Academy exhibition and developments in British painting. Drama (by Ernest Kuhe): The London stage saw new works by Bernard Shaw, J.B. Priestley, and others. The Cinema (by Monica Ewer): The 39 Steps, Mutiny on the Bounty, and British film’s growing reputation. Music (by Ernest Kuhe): The Proms season, new compositions by Vaughan Williams and Walton. Biological Sciences (by W.B. Brierley): Advances in genetics and biochemistry. Physical Sciences: Developments in nuclear physics and astronomy.

Finance and Commerce in 1935

A review of the economic year: trade improved, unemployment fell from 2.2 million to 1.8 million. The Budget showed a surplus of £7.5 million. Road transport expanded. The building industry boomed. Agricultural prices recovered modestly. The Stock Exchange rallied.

Law in 1935

Important legal decisions and legislation, including the Housing Act, the Restriction of Ribbon Development Act, and the Government of India Act. The Law of Property reforms continued.

Public Documents

I. Hitler’s Statement Announcing Universal Military Conscription in Germany (March 16) — repudiation of Part V of the Versailles Treaty. II. The British Ambassador’s Note to the German Government — protest against German rearmament. III. French Note Protesting against German Military Moves. IV. French Note to the League — referral of German violations to the Council. V. Italy’s Note to Berlin — response to German conscription. VI. Soviet-French Treaty of Mutual Assistance (May 2) — collective security pact. VII. Mr. James G. McDonald’s Letter of Resignation as League High Commissioner for Refugees — citing the failure of states to respond to the German refugee crisis.

Obituary of Eminent Persons Deceased in 1935

Notable deaths included King George V’s cousin Prince Arthur of Connaught, the statesman Lord Balfour, the composer Sir Edward Elgar, the explorer Sir Francis Younghusband, and the writer Sir James Barrie. Obituaries covered figures from politics, science, literature and the arts.
