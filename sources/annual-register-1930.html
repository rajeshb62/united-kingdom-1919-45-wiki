The Annual Register 1930

Part I — English History

Chapter I — THE COAL BILL AND THE NAVAL CONFERENCE

The close of the 1929 session had left the Labour Government in a precarious position, its fate bound up with the Coal Bill. The attack on the second reading had been warded off but the measure’s future remained uncertain. Meanwhile the London Naval Conference opened in January, with the Government seeking an agreement on cruiser ratios, culminating in the London Naval Treaty of April 22. The chapter covers the parliamentary struggle over the Coal Bill, the course of the naval negotiations, Lord Grey’s attack on Mr. Lloyd George, the state of trade, and Mr. Thomas’s attempts to secure bank credit for industry.

Chapter II — THE NEW TAXATION

The accounts for 1929-30 showed a deficit of £14.5 million. Mr. Snowden’s Budget of April 14 raised income tax to 4s 6d and increased surtax and estate duties, provoking strong criticism from Mr. Churchill and Mr. Lloyd George. The chapter covers the Budget debate, the Coal Bill’s passage through the House of Lords after a three-party compromise on hours and quotas, the Consumers’ Council Bill, the Commercial Agreement with Soviet Russia, and the I.L.P. conference.

Chapter III — FISCAL POLICY AND THE IMPERIAL CONFERENCE

The parliamentary recess saw speeches by Mr. Baldwin and Mr. Neville Chamberlain attacking Lord Beaverbrook’s ‘Empire Free Trade’ policy. The Imperial Conference opened in October, dominated by economic questions: imperial preference, the Ottawa system, and the depression in trade. The chapter covers Conservative fiscal policy, the Free Trade Manifesto, the T.U.C. Congress, the Bishops and Birth Control debate, the Mandates Commission report on Palestine, and the collapse of the Naval Treaty negotiations between Britain, the US, and France.

Chapter IV — THE LABOUR-LIBERAL CONTACT

Parliament reassembled on October 28 with a crowded programme. The Conservative vote of censure, the deepening internal split in the Liberal Party, the publication of the Liberal Memorandum on Unemployment, and the help extended to the iron and shipbuilding industries are covered. The King’s Speech included measures on education, factories, and transport. Sir Oswald Mosley’s resignation from the Government, the Liberal-Labour tension over electoral reform, and the Wreck of the R101 airship in October dominate the closing months of the year.

The Empire

Chapter I — IRELAND

Northern Ireland’s Parliament opened in March; the legislative programme included the Unemployment Insurance Bill and the Education Bill. The Irish Free State faced economic difficulties and continued political consolidation under the Cosgrave Government.

Chapter II — CANADA

The year’s two outstanding events were the General Election, which reversed political trends when R. B. Bennett’s Conservatives defeated Mackenzie King’s Liberals, and the Western wheat marketing crisis, which overshadowed the economic situation throughout the year.

Chapter III — THE UNION OF SOUTH AFRICA

South Africa was less affected than many countries by the world depression, though the collapse of the diamond and wool markets caused concern. The Native Economic Commission and the continuing operation of the mandate over South-West Africa were prominent.

Chapter IV — AUSTRALASIA

Australia faced severe economic perplexities, with falling export prices and rising unemployment. The Labour Government under Mr. Scullin struggled with the depression. New Zealand also experienced economic contraction and industrial unrest.

Chapter V — INDIA

The year was momentous: the civil disobedience movement organised by the National Congress led to widespread tumult and dislocation of trade. In June the Report of the Statutory Commission (the Simon Report) was published, recommending constitutional reforms. The Round Table Conference opened in London in November.

Foreign History

Chapter I — THE LEAGUE OF NATIONS

The tenth anniversary of the League’s foundation gave rise to a realisation of remarkable progress, but a far less confident spirit prevailed as the year went on. The Preparatory Commission for the Disarmament Conference continued its work; the Permanent Mandates Commission dealt with Palestine and Iraq; the economic depression cast its shadow over the League’s activities.

Chapter II — FRANCE AND ITALY

France participated in the Second Hague Conference on reparations and the London Naval Conference. The Briand Memorandum for a European Federal Union was circulated. In Italy, Mussolini’s Fascist regime consolidated further, with the corporative state taking shape and the Lateran Treaty ratified.

Chapter III — GERMANY AND AUSTRIA

The Müller coalition government fell over the question of unemployment insurance contributions. Chancellor Brüning took office in March, governing by emergency decree. The Young Plan was ratified by the Reichstag. In Austria, political polarisation continued with the Heimwehr movement gaining strength and the Creditanstalt crisis looming.

Chapter IV — SOVIET RUSSIA AND EASTERN EUROPE

Stalin’s First Five-Year Plan continued at full intensity, with forced collectivisation and rapid industrialisation. The trial of the ‘Industrial Party’ in Moscow signalled a new wave of purges. Poland and Romania maintained their anti-Soviet alliance; the Little Entente continued; Yugoslavia under King Alexander’s dictatorship faced Croat opposition; Turkey under Kemal advanced secularisation; Greece and Bulgaria remained unsettled.

Chapter V — LESSER STATES OF WESTERN AND NORTHERN EUROPE

Belgium faced internal tension over the language question. The Netherlands contended with economic depression and the nationalist movement in the Dutch East Indies. Spain’s Primo de Rivera dictatorship ended and the Berenguer government prepared for elections. Switzerland, the Scandinavian states, and Finland experienced varying degrees of economic difficulty.

Chapter VI — THE MIDDLE EAST

Persia under Reza Shah continued modernisation. Afghanistan stabilised after the civil war. Iraq’s treaty relationship with Britain was renegotiated. Palestine saw the continuing aftermath of the 1929 riots and the Hope Simpson Report on land settlement. Syria remained under French mandate.

Chapter VII — THE FAR EAST

China continued under the Nanking Government of Chiang Kai-shek, contending with northern secessionists, the Kuominchun, and Communist insurgency. Japan felt the impact of the world depression, and the Hamaguchi Government struggled to maintain liberal policies. The Dutch East Indies experienced nationalist agitation.

Chapter VIII — MOROCCO AND EGYPT

France took energetic steps to establish security in south-eastern Morocco near Bu Denib. Egypt continued under parliamentary government, with the Anglo-Egyptian treaty negotiations stalled and the Wafd Party in opposition.

Chapter IX — AMERICA

The year was marked by complete prostration of business and widespread unemployment in the United States, as the Great Depression deepened. President Hoover’s efforts to revive confidence included the Smoot-Hawley Tariff (enacted June 17), which provoked international retaliation. Latin American states including Argentina, Brazil, Chile, and Mexico all suffered severely from collapsing commodity prices.

Chronicle of Events in 1930

A day-by-day chronology of the principal political, diplomatic, military, industrial, and social events of the year, arranged by month. Covers domestic British developments and major international occurrences, including cabinet changes, parliamentary debates, the London Naval Conference, the R101 airship disaster, elections, treaties, and notable public occasions.

Retrospect of Literature, Science, and Art in 1930

A survey of the year’s contributions to literature (including notable books, poetry, and essays), art (by W. T. Whitley), drama (by Ernest Kuhe), the cinema (by Monica Ewer), music (by Ernest Kuhe), the biological sciences (by W. B. Brierley), and the physical sciences. Written by specialist contributors.

Public Documents

The texts of important state papers and official documents of 1930, including treaties, declarations, and diplomatic correspondence relating to the London Naval Treaty, the Imperial Conference, and other significant international engagements.

Obituary of Eminent Persons Deceased in 1930

Short biographical notices of notable British and foreign figures who died during the year, including statesmen, soldiers, writers, scientists, artists, and public benefactors. The notices are drawn principally from The Times obituary columns.
