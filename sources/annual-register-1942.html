The Annual Register for 1942

Part I — English History

Chapter I — The Fall of Singapore

Mr. Churchill’s visit to Washington at the end of 1941 continued into the first weeks of the new year, where his conversations with Mr. Roosevelt were directed to co-ordinating more closely the war efforts of Great Britain and the United States. On January 2 the two countries, along with China and the Soviet Union, signed the Declaration of the United Nations. The War Cabinet was reconstructed following the fall of Singapore to the Japanese on February 15 — the greatest military disaster in British history. The escape of the Scharnhorst and Gneisenau through the Channel added to public exasperation. Sir Stafford Cripps was sent to India with a new offer of Dominion status. The Japanese invasion of Burma led to the fall of Rangoon. In Libya, the British were driven back. Mr. Churchill demanded and received a vote of confidence.

Chapter II — The Anglo-Russian Treaty

At the beginning of April, Mr. Harry Hopkins and General Marshall came to London to discuss the military situation. The India Offer was rejected by Congress leaders. The Budget was presented, with massive increases in taxation. The Anglo-Russian Treaty of Alliance was signed in London on May 26 by Mr. Eden and Mr. Molotov. Rommel attacked in Libya, driving the British back; Tobruk fell on June 21, causing consternation in England. Mr. Churchill was again in Washington. The Eighth Army withdrew to El Alamein. A vote of censure was moved in the Commons but the Government won. The Beveridge fuel rationing plan was introduced.

Chapter III — The Demand for a Second Front

The Eighth Army made its stand at El Alamein. German advances in Russia continued, and a demand for a second front in Europe grew vociferous. An American mission arrived in England. The raid on Dieppe on August 19 cost heavy Canadian casualties but provided vital lessons. Mr. Churchill visited Moscow to inform Stalin that no second front could be opened in 1942. The King’s brother, the Duke of Kent, was killed in an air crash. The Trades Union Congress debated education policy and the second front. The Uthwatt Report on land development was published.

Chapter IV — The Allied Victory in North Africa

Parliament reassembled on September 29. A Bill for prolonging its own life for an eighth year was considered. General Montgomery’s plan of operations culminated in the decisive victory of El Alamein on October 23–24, with the pursuit of Rommel’s forces across Cyrenaica. On November 8, the Anglo-American expedition landed in North Africa (Operation Torch), changing the whole war situation. Rommel was driven from Cyrenaica and British forces entered Tunisia. The Beveridge Report on Social Insurance was published on December 1, receiving a favourable reception. The Commons condemned anti-Jewish atrocities. Rommel was further pursued, and fighting continued in Tunisia. A Minister-Resident was appointed in North-West Africa.

The Empire

Ireland

As a result of representations by Mr. de Valera, Northern Ireland was exempted from conscription at an early stage in the war. It therefore enjoyed all the economic advantages of the war industries boom while free from compulsory national service, resulting in an “unemployed roll of 17,000” despite the working class being fully employed.

Canada

Canada’s determination to marshal her total resources for the more vigorous prosecution of the war dominated all her activities throughout the year. The Speech from the Throne delivered on January 22 outlined a programme of total mobilisation.

The Union of South Africa

The year opened with a note of high hope sounded by Field-Marshal Smuts in his New Year broadcast. He reminded his listeners that the past year had been one of failures as well as great achievements, and appealed to his fellow South Africans for continued effort.

Australasia

Australia’s work for the Allied cause won high tribute from General MacArthur, the United States Commander-in-Chief in the Southern Pacific. Australia’s political history during 1942 was inextricably mingled with the general course of the World War.

India and Burma

Burma, after stubborn fighting, came under Japanese occupation. India was directly threatened with invasion and suffered air raids along her eastern shores. India’s accelerating contribution to the war effort and the fresh renown of her sons in far-flung battlefields marked the year.

Foreign History

Chapter I — The League of Nations

Throughout another difficult year of war, the League of Nations and the International Labour Organisation continued a wide range of activities, partly from Geneva and partly from war-time centres established in the United States, Canada, and elsewhere.

Chapter II — France and Italy

At the opening of 1942 the Darlan Ministry faced a new situation through the entry of the United States into the war. France remained under German occupation in the north, with the Vichy government in the south. In Italy, Mussolini’s regime continued the war alongside Germany.

Chapter III — Germany

Germany had not yet recovered from the excitement caused by the military reverses in Russia. For the first time the Army of the Third Reich had retreated, and the “final victory” over the Russians, announced in October 1941, was seen to be propaganda. The war on the Eastern Front consumed German resources at an unprecedented rate.

Chapter IV — Soviet Russia — The Baltic States — Poland — Czechoslovakia — Hungary — Rumania — Yugoslavia — Turkey — Greece — Bulgaria

Throughout 1942 the Soviet Union continued to bear the main brunt of the war of the United Nations against Nazi Germany. The German summer offensive pushed deep into the Caucasus and towards Stalingrad. Poland, Czechoslovakia, and the Balkan states suffered under Axis occupation or pressure. Turkey remained neutral. Greece was under Axis occupation.

Chapter V — Lesser States of Western and Northern Europe

Belgium, the Netherlands, Switzerland, Spain, Portugal, Denmark, Sweden, Norway, and Finland each navigated the pressures of war. Belgium and the Netherlands steadfastly refused participation in the German “New Order.” Switzerland maintained its neutrality. Finland continued its “Continuation War” alongside Germany against the Soviet Union. Sweden remained neutral.

Chapter VI — The Middle East

On January 29 a treaty was signed at Teheran between Iran on the one side and Great Britain and the Soviet Union on the other. Iraq, Palestine, Transjordan, Syria and Lebanon, and Arabia each faced the pressures of war, with Palestine remaining a centre of tension.

Chapter VII — The Far East

The year opened with a brilliant military success for China, as the Japanese advance on Changsha was repelled. Japan consolidated its conquests in Malaya, Burma, and the Philippines, establishing the so-called “Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere.” Thailand aligned itself with Japan.

Chapter VIII — Morocco and Egypt

On February 6 a bomb exploded on the quay at Tangier. Egypt, though nominally independent, was the base for the British Eighth Army and the scene of the decisive battles of El Alamein.

Chapter IX — America

The year 1942 opened in a very unpromising fashion for the United States. The Japanese lightning offensive in the Pacific had devastated American naval power at Pearl Harbor. However, the American war economy rapidly mobilised, and the tide began to turn with the naval victories of the Coral Sea and Midway. The United States became the “arsenal of democracy,” and the first American forces landed in North Africa in November.

Part II

Chronicle of Events in 1942

A day-by-day record of events from January to December, covering the war and home front in Britain and abroad.

Retrospect of Literature, Art, and Science

Literature, Art, Drama, the Cinema, Music, and the Biological and Physical Sciences were surveyed. Notable works included the continuing literary output under war conditions. The cinema saw the release of Mrs. Miniver and other wartime classics.

Finance and Commerce in 1942

A survey of the war economy, including war finance, taxation, industrial production, and trade under the conditions of total war.

Law in 1942

Legal developments including war-related legislation, prize court decisions, and emergency regulation cases.
